Other projection types

Newman projections

Newman questions like your worksheet require you to identify which carbon is front, which is behind, and which substituents belong to each. Convert the projection to a wedge/dash drawing first and analyze its center in the molecule editor. Automated Newman-to-R/S conversion is not yet implemented.

Chair conformations

For two chair drawings, compare atom connectivity and up/down stereochemistry, not the axial/equatorial positions alone. A chair flip can produce a different conformation of the same stereoisomer. Automated chair input and conversion are not yet implemented.

Enantiomeric excess & optical rotation

Forward calculation

Given the fraction of the pure reference enantiomer, calculate optical rotation and ee.

Equations

ee (%) = αmixture / αpure reference × 100

% reference = (100 + signed ee)/2

% opposite = (100 − signed ee)/2

αmixture = αpure × (2f − 1)

Here f is the fraction of the pure reference enantiomer, and signed ee is positive when that enantiomer is in excess. Absolute ee is |signed ee|. Requires the same experimental conditions for specific-rotation comparison.

Worksheet example: pure quinine [α] = −165°. For a mixture measured at −83°, signed ee is +50.3%, corresponding to 75.15% quinine and 24.85% its enantiomer.

Important: this is an educational tool. Simple tetrahedral CIP and stereoisomer calculations use RDKit.js; stereochemical edge cases, non-tetrahedral chirality, and molecular names may require independent verification. Chemistry libraries and PubChem lookups require an internet connection.
